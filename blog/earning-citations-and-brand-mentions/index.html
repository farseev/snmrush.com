Earning citations and brand mentions in AI answers

7 Sep 2026 · 7 min read · SNMRush editorial team · FAQ

To earn citations and brand mentions in AI answers, publish the clearest source on questions your audience genuinely asks, then get credible independent sites to describe you accurately in your category. AI assistants that search the web tend to cite pages that answer directly and draw descriptions from several sources, so mentions you do not control matter.

What is the difference between a citation and a mention?

The two are related but not the same, and it helps to track them separately.

  • Citation: an AI answer links to or names your page as a source. Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Gemini and Google AI Overviews all show sources in some form.
  • Mention: the answer names your brand, with or without a link. "Agencies such as X and Y offer this."
  • Description: how the answer characterises you. A mention with the wrong description can do more harm than no mention.

The M in SNMRush stands for Mentions because we think this third surface is the most underrated. Search results and neural answers both increasingly reflect what others say about you.

Why do third-party mentions matter so much?

We should be careful here: no AI company publishes how its systems choose sources. But a few things are well established. Assistants that browse retrieve several pages and synthesise them. When a question is comparative ("best", "alternatives to", "which agency"), the pages retrieved are often lists, reviews, forums and articles written by people other than the brands themselves. If you only appear on your own site, you are one voice in a room where the synthesis favours consensus.

Language models also learn associations during training from text across the web. A brand repeatedly described in a category, by varied credible sources, is more likely to be associated with that category. Neither mechanism rewards volume of links in the old SEO sense. Both reward accurate, repeated, independent description.

How do you earn mentions without spamming?

Here is the playbook we run, roughly in order of effort.

  1. Fix what you already have. Search for your brand and list every page that describes you. Correct outdated directory listings, partner pages and old press boilerplate. This is the quickest win available.
  2. Be useful where your audience asks. Communities, forums and professional groups are where comparative questions get answered in public. Answer genuinely, as a named person, and disclose your affiliation.
  3. Publish something worth citing. Original frameworks, checklists, definitions and clear explanations of recurring problems. Journalists and bloggers link to the source that saves them work.
  4. Show up in people's formats. Podcasts, panels, webinars and community events create transcripts, show notes and listings that describe you in context.
  5. Pursue relevant comparison and review pages. Ask to be assessed fairly, provide accurate information, and accept the result.

What we avoid: paid link schemes, fake reviews, mass-produced guest posts and planting brand names in forums. Beyond the ethics, these tactics are increasingly detectable and can damage how both search engines and people see you.

Bigger than most SEO plans allow. A talk at a meetup produces an event listing, a recording, social posts from attendees and, often, a write-up. Each describes you in context. Mention-earning checklist

  • Inventory of every page that describes your brand, with errors logged.
  • A canonical description supplied to partners and directories.
  • Three to five evidence-backed topics your audience genuinely cares about.
  • One citable asset per topic: a framework, guide or clear explainer.
  • A named spokesperson active in the relevant communities.
  • A quarterly prompt panel to see which sources AI answers cite in your category.

How do you see which sources AI answers rely on?

Run your category's comparative questions through Perplexity, ChatGPT with search, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, and record every source shown. Patterns appear quickly: the same few review sites, publications or forums tend to recur in a category. Those are your mention targets. Mentions are slow to earn and hard to fake. That is exactly why they are worth more than ever: in an answer engine, being described well by others is the closest thing to a recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Do backlinks still matter for AI visibility?

Links still matter for search rankings, which many AI answers draw on. For AI descriptions, accurate unlinked mentions on credible sites also appear to matter, so track both.

How can I see which sites AI tools cite in my category?

Run a consistent set of category questions through Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Gemini and AI Overviews and log every source shown. Recurring sources are your priority targets.

Is it acceptable to mention my brand in forums?

Yes, if you answer genuinely, as a named person, and disclose your affiliation. Planting anonymous recommendations is deceptive and usually backfires.
