Standing out when AI makes every brand sound the same

27 Jul 2026 · 6 min read · SNMRush editorial team · FAQ

When AI tools let every brand publish the same competent paragraphs, the content that earns citations is the content that adds something others do not: first-hand evidence, a clear point of view, specific examples and the audience's own language. Answer engines synthesise many sources, so a page that repeats the consensus gives them little reason to name you.

Why does sameness hurt visibility now?

Think about how an AI answer is assembled. A system retrieves several pages that address a question, then writes a synthesis. If ten pages say the same thing in slightly different words, the synthesis needs only one of them, and it may need none, because the consensus is already well represented in what the model learned. The pages that get named are often the ones that contributed something distinct: a definition, a framework, a figure, a caveat, an example.

We are careful not to overstate the mechanics; no AI company publishes its source-selection rules. But Google's guidance on helpful content has long emphasised originality and first-hand expertise, and the logic of synthesis points the same way. Repeating the consensus is a weak position.

What does differentiated content actually contain?

  • Evidence you hold and others do not: what your audience says, what your support team hears, what you have tested.
  • A point of view: a claim someone could disagree with, argued honestly.
  • Specific examples: worked cases, even hypothetical ones, that show the idea applied.
  • Your audience's language: the words customers use, which are rarely the words in your brand guidelines.
  • Honest limits: when your approach is the wrong choice. Few brands say this, which is why it stands out.

Where does the evidence come from?

Most brands have more of it than they think, scattered across sales notes, reviews and social threads. The trouble is turning it into something structured and trustworthy.

That is the job SOMIN, an AI audience-research platform, does for SNMRush. SOMIN analyses real social conversation and identifies the tensions people express, from frustration to aspiration, with every finding linked to the posts behind it. Our listening on brand differentiation keeps surfacing the same tension among marketers: the pressure to publish more with AI, and the fear that it makes them indistinguishable from everyone else. Discounting and volume are the usual responses. Neither builds a reason to be quoted.

We treat that evidence as the raw material of differentiation. If customers in your category keep asking a question nobody has answered well, answering it is your point of difference. A differentiation checklist for every page

  1. What does this page say that the top five results do not?
  2. Which piece of evidence here is ours alone?
  3. Is there a claim a competitor would disagree with?
  4. Does it use at least three phrases taken from how the audience speaks?
  5. Is there a worked example?
  6. Does it say when this advice does not apply?

If a page fails most of these, it may still rank for a while. It is unlikely to be the source an assistant chooses to name.

Definitions

  • Information gain: the new information a page adds beyond what similar pages already provide. The term is widely used in SEO; treat it as a useful idea rather than a confirmed ranking factor.
  • Consensus content: content that restates what is already widely published.
  • First-hand evidence: data, observations or experience gathered directly, rather than summarised from others.

Is AI-assisted content a problem in itself?

No. Google has said it judges content on quality rather than how it was produced, and most good teams use AI somewhere in their process. The problem is unedited, ungrounded AI content: fluent, plausible and empty. Our sister consultancy GPT3 Marketing has worked on brand voice systems since the GPT-3 API opened in 2020, and their position is one we share: AI is a drafting tool, and evidence plus human editing is what makes the result yours.

The same holds for agentic workflows. If you automate research and drafting, the quality of what goes in decides the quality of what comes out. Agencies in the SAMY case study illustrate how grounding work in audience evidence shapes the creative that follows.

How does this connect to SEO, AEO and GEO?

Differentiation is not a separate workstream. It sits inside each of them. In search, distinct content is more likely to earn links and engagement. In answer engines, a distinct, clearly stated answer is easier to select. In generative engines, a distinct contribution is more likely to be named in a synthesis. Our overview of SEO, AEO and GEO shows how the three fit together.

What should you do this quarter?

Pick your ten most important pages. Run the checklist above on each. For every page that fails, find one piece of evidence only you hold, ideally from real audience conversation, and rebuild the page around it. Then track whether AI answers in your category start to mention you, using the method in measuring AI visibility.

A useful exercise: ask an AI assistant to write your next article before you do. Whatever it produces is the consensus. Your job is to write the version it could not, because it lacks your evidence and your audience's voice.

You do not stand out by shouting louder or publishing more. You stand out by knowing something about your audience that others have not bothered to learn, and saying it plainly.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google penalise AI-generated content?

Google says it evaluates content on quality and helpfulness, not on how it was produced. Mass-produced content made to manipulate rankings is a problem whatever the method.

What is information gain in SEO?

An informal term for the new information a page adds beyond similar pages. It is a useful editorial test, though no engine has confirmed it as a named ranking factor.

How can a small brand differentiate its content?

Use evidence you already hold: customer questions, support tickets and social conversation. Answer the questions bigger competitors ignore, in your audience's own words.
