Entity building: how to make AI engines know who you are

14 Sep 2026 · 7 min read · SNMRush editorial team · FAQ

Entity building is the work of making your brand a clear, unambiguous "thing" that search engines and AI models recognise, with consistent facts about who you are, what you do and how you relate to other entities. It combines on-site clarity, structured data, official profiles and independent third-party sources that describe you the same way.

What is an entity, and why do AI engines care?

Search engines stopped thinking only in strings of words years ago. They try to understand things: companies, people, products, places, and the relationships between them. Google's Knowledge Graph is the best-known example. Large language models build something looser but similar during training: associations between a name and the facts that tend to appear near it.

When someone asks an assistant "which agencies help with AI search visibility in Singapore?", the answer depends partly on which entities the system associates with that category and place, and partly on what it finds when it searches the web. If your brand is fuzzy, inconsistent or easily confused with another, you are less likely to be named, and more likely to be described wrongly when you are.

What are the signs of a weak entity?

  • Assistants describe your company using an old positioning, a former name or a competitor's features.
  • Your brand is confused with a similarly named company. (We think about this a lot: SNMRush stands for Search, Neural answers and Mentions, and has no connection to any SEO software vendor.)
  • Your about page, LinkedIn, directory listings and press boilerplate all describe you differently.
  • Nobody outside your own site writes about you in your category.

How do you build a strong brand entity?

We work through five layers, in order.

1. Write your canonical description

One or two sentences that name the brand, the category, the audience and the location. Then use it, word for word or close to it, everywhere you control. Our own: "SNMRush is an SEO, AEO and GEO agency that helps brands rank in search, get cited in AI answers and earn brand mentions." It is not poetic. It is repeatable.

2. Make the home of the entity unmistakable

Your about page should answer the obvious questions in plain prose: what you are, who founded you, where you operate, what you are not. Mark it up with Organization schema and sameAs links, as covered in our guide to structured data for AI search.

3. Align the profiles you own

LinkedIn, YouTube, Crunchbase, Google Business Profile, app stores, industry directories. Same name, same description, same logo, same URL. This is tedious, and it is exactly the tedium most competitors skip.

4. Earn independent descriptions

Entities are strengthened by sources you do not control: trade press, partner pages, podcasts, conference listings, reputable reviews, and community discussion. Each one that describes you accurately and in your category reinforces the association. We cover how to earn them in our article on citations and brand mentions.

5. Own a set of questions

The most durable entity signal is being the best source on a set of questions in your space. That is where audience research comes in.

Why do questions matter more than keywords for entities?

Because AI engines are built to answer questions, and the associations they form are about which sources reliably answer which questions. A brand that publishes the clearest explanation of a recurring audience problem becomes linked to that problem.

Entity checklist

  1. Canonical one-sentence description agreed and documented.
  2. About page answers what, who, where and what-not, in prose.
  3. Organization schema with stable @id, logo and sameAs links.
  4. All owned profiles aligned on name, description and URL.
  5. Named people (founders, authors) with consistent bios and profiles.
  6. At least a handful of independent sources describing you accurately.
  7. A cluster of pages that answer your audience's real questions better than anyone else.
  8. A quarterly check of how AI assistants describe you, with errors logged and traced to sources.

How long does entity building take?

Owned changes can be done in weeks. Search engines may pick them up as soon as pages are recrawled. Language models are slower: what a model "knows" from training changes only when a new version is trained, though assistants that search the web can reflect updates sooner. Independent mentions accumulate over months. Plan for a quarter to see consistent description, and longer for strong category association.

Be wary of anyone who promises to "get you into the knowledge graph" in days, or to make an assistant say specific things about you. Nobody controls that. What you can control is the clarity and consistency of the evidence.

What does good look like?

Ask three assistants what your company does. If they give a correct, consistent answer, name the right category, and do not confuse you with anyone else, your entity is in good shape. The Reassured case study is a useful read on how a brand grounded its positioning in audience evidence; that kind of clarity is what entity work makes visible to machines.

Entity building rarely feels urgent. It is the work underneath every other visibility effort, and when it is missing, everything else leaks.

Frequently asked questions

What is a brand entity?

A brand entity is your company as a uniquely identifiable thing that search engines and AI systems recognise, connected to consistent facts such as category, location, people and official profiles.

Can I edit what ChatGPT says about my company?

Not directly. You can make the evidence clearer and more consistent across your site and independent sources, and assistants that search the web may reflect it sooner than trained model knowledge.

Do I need a Wikipedia page for entity SEO?

No. Wikipedia has strict notability rules and should not be used for promotion. Consistent owned profiles, structured data and genuine third-party coverage are the practical route.
